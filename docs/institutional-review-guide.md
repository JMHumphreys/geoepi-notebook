# Institutional review guide

This guide helps human reviewers assess the GeoEpi Lab Book. It does not approve the guidance, assign institutional authority, establish retention periods, or replace applicable law, contracts, agreements, privacy rules, records schedules, repository terms, or community governance.

For every question, distinguish the following:

- **Scientific guidance:** what helps make the work understandable and scientifically defensible.
- **Proposed lab minimum:** a practice the Lab may choose to expect after review.
- **Institutional authority:** the office, committee, agreement owner, repository, or community authority that must decide where applicable; do not infer a role from this guide.
- **Unresolved decision:** a question that needs an explicit institutional answer.
- **Optional stronger practice:** a proportionate improvement that is not universal.

## Scientific-method review

Ask ecologists, epidemiologists, statisticians, modelers, GIS specialists, and field scientists:

- Are study questions, estimands, case definitions, sampling designs, spatial and temporal support, model assumptions, scenarios, uncertainty, and intended uses represented accurately?
- Do the Plan, Analyze, and Validate boundaries distinguish producing an output from supporting an interpretation?
- Are negative, inconclusive, failed, exploratory, and sensitivity results handled without implying that they are invalid or publishable by default?
- Can a reader trace a result to the dataset version, code, settings, run, validation evidence, and communication product when that trace is consequential?
- Are examples scientifically balanced across field studies, surveillance, raster analysis, models, simulations, genomics, sensors, and operational forecasts?

Reviewers should identify proposed minimum information, not prescribe one model, metric, design, software, or file structure. Questions about scientific adequacy remain with the responsible scientific reviewers and project authorities.

## Data and privacy review

Ask data stewards, privacy and security reviewers, agreement owners, and relevant custodians:

- Are source identity, acquisition, restrictions, consent or agreement conditions, sensitive locations, identifiers, transformations, and access classifications recorded?
- Does the guidance distinguish restricted preservation from public access and avoid treating FAIR as unrestricted access?
- Do templates avoid credentials, encryption keys, protected data, and unauthorized transfers?
- Are legal holds, unresolved agreements, community authority, and uncertain retention requirements treated as stop conditions?
- Are repository selection and access-review responsibilities explicit without naming an unapproved repository?

The Lab Book can propose recording restrictions and authority references. Institutional or agreement owners decide classification, access, retention, transfer, and disposal. No field in a template authorizes disclosure or destruction.

## Collaboration and credit review

Ask project leads, collaborators, partner organizations, and community authorities:

- Are roles, decision authority, contribution records, credit, disagreement, partner conditions, and handoff responsibilities clear?
- Can a partner or community authority change access, use, attribution, or preservation conditions over time?
- Do the Collaborate and Communicate stages distinguish readiness to communicate from release authorization?
- Are incoming maintainers and repository custodians required to accept responsibility rather than being assumed?

Reviewers may propose a lab minimum for durable decisions and contribution records. Actual authority follows project agreements, institutional rules, partner terms, and community governance.

## Communication and release review

Ask publication, communications, operational, and release authorities:

- Are product preparation, claim review, release-review readiness, release decision, release state, correction, withdrawal, and supersession distinct?
- Does release apply to one exact product version, audience, channel, purpose, and set of conditions?
- Are availability statements honest when source data are controlled or software dependencies are proprietary?
- Do correction and withdrawal records preserve historical identity without automatically requiring destruction?
- Is the distinction between public release and preservation clear?

The release record is authoritative for release decisions and events. Publication, operational, privacy, contractual, and institutional authorities determine approval. The Lab Book does not turn a draft product into an approved release.

## Preservation and records review

Ask repository, records-management, data-management, and preservation specialists:

- Are selection, nonselection, deferral, package contents, deposit, verification, access, migration, maintenance, deaccession, and supersession distinct?
- Does the guidance identify retention or disposition authority without inventing a period?
- Do rejection, deaccession, and supersession records retain evidence and historical relationships?
- Are repository receipt, accepted contents, transformations, identifiers, checksums, retrieval tests, and limitations recorded proportionately?
- Are storage, recovery, migration, environmental cost, continuing fees, and repository exit responsibilities visible?

The preservation plan and record is authoritative for selection and outcome; the package manifest is authoritative for contents. Applicable records schedules, legal holds, contracts, repositories, and community authorities take precedence. A checksum is integrity evidence, not scientific validation; a backup is not automatically preservation.

## Accessibility and usability review

Ask scientists using the site and people reviewing accessible presentation:

- Can a reader find guidance for starting a project, receiving data, preparing analysis-ready data, recording a run, tracing a result, validating uncertainty, coordinating contributors, releasing work, and preserving it?
- Are headings, tables, callouts, Mermaid diagrams, long status values, raw-template links, and external links understandable at narrow widths?
- Are links descriptive, keyboard usable, and visibly distinct from ordinary text?
- Are scientific examples and terminology understandable without software-development or archival training?
- Are optional stronger practices clearly distinguished from proposed minimums?

This review should document observed barriers and agreed improvements. It should not claim full conformance unless a qualified accessibility review establishes it.
